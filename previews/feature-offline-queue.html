Offline-first field reliability

Not a new capability — a repair. The portal already claims to save logs offline and send them later. It does not reliably do either, and when it fails it fails silently.

These are mockups, not working screens. The code panel below, however, is the real current implementation, quoted from js/features/service-log.js.
Screen 1What happens today

This runs whenever a technician submits a visit with no signal, or when the request to Apps Script fails. It is eight lines long and it has two defects.

Defect 1 · Overwrite

One fixed key. Log Mendoza with no signal, drive to Moczygemba and log that too — Mendoza is gone. No error, no warning. The tech was told both were saved.

Defect 2 · Photos dropped

Photos travel as base64 inside the payload. localStorage caps near 5 MB. Five photos is roughly 7 MB, so the quota throws on a normal visit — and the catch block's answer is to save it with no photos at all.

Defect 3 · Retry can duplicate

The log UID is minted server-side. A queued job that actually succeeded but timed out gets re-sent as a fresh row. The 5-minute same-pool dedup catches this only inside that window — a log flushed hours later is not protected.

Why this matters more than it sounds

The before/after photos are the evidence. They go into the customer's visit report email, they are what you reach for when someone disputes whether the pool was serviced, and under Phase 3 they would be what the customer sees in their own portal. They are also the one part of the record that cannot be reconstructed from memory — a tech can re-enter chemistry, they cannot re-photograph a pool they left an hour ago.

South Texas backyards, block walls, pool equipment pads behind the house. Dead zones are routine, not exceptional.

Screens 2 & 3The queue, made visible

A real durable queue in IndexedDB — many jobs, photos held as Blobs instead of base64 text, surviving a reload, a force-quit and a dead battery. And the technician can see it, which is most of the fix: the current design's worst property is that it is invisible.

What has to be handled carefully

There is no bundler and no content hashing in this project, so a service worker has nothing to cache-bust against. A careless one can pin every technician to a stale build until they clear site data — which in the field is unrecoverable.

The guard is a versioned cache name plus explicit skipWaiting, and a visible “update available — tap to reload” bar rather than a silent swap.

Leaflet is currently pulled from a CDN at index.html:13. It has to be self-hosted or the map is blank the moment the tech is offline — which is exactly when they need it.

Under itBefore and after

The whole change, stated plainly.

Today
  • One localStorage key — job two silently destroys job one
  • Quota failure is “handled” by discarding every photo
  • ~5 MB ceiling, shared with all other portal caching
  • Queue is invisible — the tech has no way to confirm anything
  • Server-minted UID, so a timed-out retry can duplicate a row
  • Nothing loads at all without a connection
After
  • IndexedDB queue, unlimited jobs, each independently tracked
  • Photos stored as Blobs — no base64 inflation, no quota wall
  • Hundreds of megabytes available, isolated from other caches
  • Queue visible in the hub with per-job status and retry
  • Client-minted UID — re-sending a landed job is a no-op
  • App shell and today's route render offline; installable
One migration detail. Any technician's phone may be holding a log in the old svc_queued_submit key right now. First load after the update drains that key into the new queue before anything else runs, then clears it — so nothing in flight is lost on the way over.

Where it touches the code: a new js/lib/offline-queue.js; a rewrite of _svcQueueAndBail_, _onSvcOnline_ and _retrySvcNow_ in service-log.js; acceptance of _client_uid in the submit_form handler, checked against the _log_uid column that already exists; a new sw.js and manifest.json; and self-hosting Leaflet.
