Four things worth building next

A review of the portal — the SPA, the Apps Script backend, and the Sheets behind it — looking for the highest-value thing to build next. These are the four candidates, each sketched screen by screen.

Feature sketches · August 2026

How these were chosen

The portal is mature. Routing, scheduling, the service log with photos, chemical costing, margins, payroll with QuickBooks journal entries, quotes through to e-signed agreements, onboarding, training, inventory and customer email all work today. So the question was not what's missing in the abstract — it was where the gap between what the system already knows and what it does with that knowledge is widest.

Three real gaps came out of it, plus one candidate that turned out to be mostly built already. Each card below states the problem with the file and line that demonstrates it, so none of this rests on my opinion of your business.

These are mockups. No portal code was modified to produce them — every file here lives in previews/, exactly like the quote and agreement email previews.

1Recommended

Pool Health Radar

Chemistry intelligence from data you already collect — a chlorine target that's right for each specific pool, and a ranked list of the pools about to fail.

Every visit records six readings, water color, algae, equipment status and photos. All of it is used for costing and none of it for prediction. Nothing in the codebase relates chlorine to cyanuric acid — so a pool at CYA 90 shows the same “3–5 IDEAL” band as one at CYA 30, though it needs roughly triple the chlorine to sanitize. That is the most common reason a pool on a paid weekly route still turns green.

2Active data loss

Offline-first field reliability

Not a new capability — a repair. The portal claims to save logs offline and send them later. It reliably does neither, and it fails silently.

Queued visits go to one localStorage key, so a second pool logged with no signal destroys the first. And because photos ride as base64 inside a ~5 MB bucket, a normal five-photo visit blows the quota — at which point the catch block re-saves the log with the photos removed. The technician is shown “LOG SAVED.”

3Biggest upside

Customer portal

A passwordless page where customers see their own pool — every visit, photo and reading — instead of calling the office to ask.

Customers get transactional email and have nowhere to look anything up. Most of the plumbing already exists: photo URLs are already stored per visit, and buildVisitReportPayload_() already assembles this exact view for the report email. The token pattern is already working for e-signatures. This is largely assembly — but it is also the first surface anyone outside the company can reach.

Route drive-time optimization

Included because you asked to see all four. When I went looking for what to build, I found the optimizer already running in production.

Every technician's day is already nearest-neighbor ordered using real cached driving minutes, before the schedule reaches the browser. What's genuinely left is a 2-opt refinement worth about 6%, pinned stops for gate codes and time windows — and one thing worth taking on its own: the drive-time numbers are computed and then thrown away, so nobody can see route quality at all.

If it were my call

Ranked by value per day of work, not by how interesting they are to build.

1
Pool Health Radar

Largest gain per day of work, and it monetizes data you are already paying to collect. Green pools cost a re-clean, a chemical burn, an apology, and sometimes the customer. The CYA relationship is genuinely non-obvious — this creates operational lift, not just another dashboard.

Build first
2
Offline-first field reliability

Ranked on urgency rather than upside. This isn't a missing feature, it's a defect deleting photos from completed visits right now. Photos are the one part of the record that cannot be reconstructed — a technician can re-enter chemistry, they cannot re-photograph a pool they left an hour ago.

Fix soon
3
Customer portal

The biggest business case and the biggest build. Worth doing — but worth doing after the two above, because it opens a public surface and adds ongoing support load, and neither of those is a good thing to take on while photos are still going missing.

Then this
4
Route optimization

Skip for now, with one carve-out: surfacing the drive-time numbers that already exist is a couple of hours and gives you a way to see route quality degrade and to price distant pools correctly. The 2-opt pass can wait until routes are big enough that 6% is worth chasing.

Decisions that need you, not me

  • The chlorine ratios. The 7.5% / 15% / 40% figures in sketch 1 are the standard industry numbers, not MCPS policy. They'd live in one tunable block. Tell me the numbers you want and those are the numbers that ship.
  • Whether LSI ships at all. It needs a water temperature you don't currently log. The plan is an optional field with a forecast-derived estimate, clearly labelled — but the whole thing can be dropped and the rest still works.
  • Drive photo permissions. Visit photos are saved as ANYONE_WITH_LINK. Fine today; worth deciding before customers hold portal links in sketch 3.
  • Referral terms. The offer shown in sketch 3 is illustrative. That's a business decision.
